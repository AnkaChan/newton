# LeCO digest for LIDO (2026-09-26)

Source: 94 confirmed candidates from seven readers of the LearnedClothOptimizer checkout, each verified against LeCO code, LIDO code and the LIDO plan (workflow output `wlhelzjt9`). This file merges them into 25 themes, then lists negative lessons, rejected or already-present items, and a suggested order.

## Summary (read this if nothing else)

1. The biggest structural difference between LeCO and LIDO is the step controller: LeCO keeps one recurrent per-object log step with asymmetric grow/shrink, a clamp, and feedback of the state as an input (LeCO core/model.py:625-645, 2098-2131); LIDO uses a stateless per-cell sigmoid step (network.py:354) chosen by Anka on 2026-09-26, so everything built on LeCO's controller (theme 1, the trust contraction in theme 9) needs her decision.
2. The cheapest high-value items are diagnostics LIDO lacks: step-head saturation fractions, input clip fractions, applied-update RMS, per-update uphill fraction, per-block input RMS (theme 2, train_mixed.py and network.py) and the parameter-gradient norm (theme 12). None touches a recorded decision.
3. Evaluation gaps: LIDO has no classical baseline on its own objective (theme 22, mixed_validation.py), no iteration-level oscillation metrics (theme 18), no jerk or edge-ratio tail metrics (theme 24), and selects checkpoints at K=100 although training K is at most 32 (theme 19).
4. Numerics: the raw log gradient RMS scalar (input_assembly.py:265; walkthrough R7, -27.6 for a zero gradient) is the one input LeCO standardizes with frozen calibration statistics (theme 4); LeCO's negative lesson forbids running statistics on it.
5. Training-loop robustness: LeCO masks a nonfinite sample and resets that environment instead of aborting the run (theme 11); adopting that in LIDO (train_mixed.py, trajectory_pool.py) changes the recorded "nonfinite is an explicit failure" rule.
6. Energy-monotonic acceptance in training (theme 9) is LeCO's hard-regime tool, off in its main training; the evidence is fine-tune only, and half of it (trust contraction) depends on a controller LIDO does not have.
7. Data distribution: uniform K/H draws (trajectory_pool.py:189-190) make an H=128, K=32 trajectory contribute 512x the queries of a (1,8) one (theme 14); LeCO bounded long-horizon share with slot caps. Contradicts Q13.
8. Several LeCO items are lessons only: per-vertex steps caused strain, widening the step bound saturated it, a global token bypassed the saturated controller, a fixed long horizon hurt rollouts, multiscale attention improved residuals but not rollouts (Negative lessons section).
9. Ten candidates were rejected (already present, misdescribed, or constraint-only) and 65 of the 94 were merged as duplicates or sub-items of another candidate; see the section near the end.
10. Suggested first moves: themes 2, 12, 18, 19, 8 (diagnostics and cheap reporting), then 22 (classical baseline), then the flagged ablations once diagnostics show a trigger.

## Themes

### 1. Step controller: recurrent per-object log step, per-object vs per-cell, gradient through the bound

Merged: C1 [0], L1 per-vertex strain [12], C14 [72], C7 hard-bound lesson [73], C01-recurrent-step-controller [76], remedy half of C2 [1].

- What LeCO does: `OptimizerState` carries `last_gradient, last_update, log_scale` with `log_scale` of shape [B,1,1] (core/model.py:625-645). Each Cell mean-pools the tokens, emits tanh(scale_head), maps it through `asymmetric_log_scale_delta` (growth 1.0, shrink 1.5; zero takes the growth branch so the zero-initialized head keeps a nonzero derivative, core/model.py:562-579), adds it to the previous log_scale, clamps to [ln 1e-5, ln 5e-3] m, and `update = log_scale_next.exp() * direction` (core/model.py:2098-2131; configs/hood_vto.yaml:42-46). The normalized state `((log_scale - log(initial)) / span).clamp(-1, 1)` is an input channel (core/model.py:1592-1599). The state persists across Cells and frames (rollout.py:1027-1030; core/sequence.py:2027-2037). Correction: the per-query shrink is exp(-1.5), about 4.5x; growth exp(1.0) = 2.7x. The comment "Per-vertex scales let neighboring vertices choose inconsistent step lengths and were a direct source of artificial strain" (core/model.py:2098-2101) and the per-frame-reset failure (scale reset to 1 mm each frame then jumping to 3.9 mm inside the frame, astra 2026-07-23.md:1233) are the recorded reasons; both exist as comment or log, not as ablation data. The clamp is hard in hood_vto.yaml:50; only ccraft_multilayer_300hz.yaml:49 uses `straight_through_clamp` (`value + (clamped - value).detach()`, core/model.py:581-586) so a controller saturated above 5 mm can still learn to shrink (config comment :47-48; README.md:151-153); upstream later deleted the option (astra 2026-09-09.md:670).
- Why it matters for LIDO: the step is `max_step_size * sigmoid(step_head(features))` per cell with no memory (network.py:354; bound 0.05 at train_mixed.py:62); notes/v1-DECISIONS.MD:1176-1177 recorded it saturated at 0.05, where the sigmoid gradient vanishes. A multiplicative controller shrinks after an uphill query and grows while descending, which is how LeCO runs 30 Cells per frame over long rollouts. LIDO's fusion turns neighbour inconsistency into fit residual rather than edge strain, so the strain risk is lower than in LeCO but unmeasured.
- LIDO adaptation: (a) diagnostic first: log the RMS of `step_size[i] - step_size[j]` over hop-1 neighbours and the fusion residual (requested minus achieved axis change) per epoch in train_mixed.py (optimizer_probe.py:111-143 already computes the fusion residual). (b) ablation: `step_mode in {per_cell, per_object, recurrent}` in `IntrinsicSolverNetwork.forward` (network.py:335-356); the recurrent variant adds `log_step` [B] to `OptimizerHistory` (input_assembly.py:92-110), `HISTORY_KEYS` (history.py:28), `empty_history` and `carry_history`, and a scalar in `features.SCALAR_FEATURES` (schema bump); pooled head, tanh, asymmetric delta, add, clamp, exp. Keep the radial correction bound (network.py:353). (c) if theme 2 shows pinning, a straight-through alternative `max * straight_through_clamp(0.5 + 0.25*z, 0, 1)` matches the sigmoid value and slope at z=0 but makes the interval closed [0, max]. Also fix notes/decision-review.md:170, which still says "one object-wide sigmoid scale".
- Effort: small for the diagnostic; medium for the ablation (retraining, schema bump).
- Flag: needs Anka's decision / ablation. Contradicts the per-cell sigmoid step decision of 2026-09-26 (v2-plan.md:143-149 and 673-674: "No other step controller or output parametrization was selected"). The per-object option is exactly what she replaced; the notes record the decision but no rationale, so ask before proposing the run. Do not justify it through rejection contraction (theme 9), which separately collides with inversion acceptance.

### 2. Forward diagnostics: saturation, clip fractions, applied update, uphill fraction, per-block input RMS

Merged: diagnostic half of C2 [1], C11 [10], C10 [23], L2 uphill-fraction diagnostic [39], C6 [68], C02-step-saturation-diagnostics [77], C07-feature-block-rms-logging [81].

- What LeCO does: `forward` fills `last_forward_diagnostics` (core/model.py:2156) with 35 scalars when `collect_diagnostics` is on (default, :1401): clip fractions of the three RMS-normalized history blocks (:1428-1445), passthrough clip fraction and max (:1583-1591), raw direction norm and `direction_component_saturation_fraction` (|d| > 0.95, :2048-2062), `scale_controller_saturation_fraction` (|tanh| > 0.95, :2104-2110), `scale_at_minimum/maximum_fraction`, `raw_scale_below/above_maximum_fraction`, `update_norm_mean/max_m` (:2134-2153). train.py:927-936 writes eight of them into every record; scripts/summarize_training_runs.py:92-140 derives `direct_energy_decrease_fraction` and windowed distributions; train.py:975-981 computes `applied_update_rms` and :1142 records it as `applied_update_rms_m`. Correction: the history-block clip fractions and the low-side scale indicators are computed but not written by training (train.py:927-936 selects eight base keys); they surface only through the rollout diagnostics path (rollout.py:1321-1331). Use in practice: the widened model sat at the 5 mm cap 87.9% of the time with 85.9% controller saturation against 2.44% for the baseline (astra 2026-08-12.md:2190-2200); an ablation without log RMS(|g|) saturated 100% (astra 2026-09-09.md:1481). In one comparison both models sat at the cap about equally, so log the controller pre-activation saturation as well as the at-cap fraction. Per-block input statistics: LeCO logged body-motion feature means (astra models/optimizer.py:1254-1262) yet a refactor that zeroed that input went unnoticed for two to three days until a regression assertion was added (astra 2026-08-26.md:902-951); the assertion, not the log, closed the gap.
- Why it matters for LIDO: train_mixed.py:744-757 logs only step_size mean/min/max, tie count and residual. The recorded LIDO failure modes (step saturation at 0.05, the R7 log-RMS outlier, the R11 previous-gradient block at the +/-10 clip) were all found by hand probes. Mean loss is not a progress signal (the asinh form sits near asinh(1) = 0.881, rejected candidate C05), so an uphill count is the honest per-update descent measure; LIDO's `descent_rate` (mixed_validation.py:467) covers only the first update.
- LIDO adaptation: in `IntrinsicSolverNetwork.forward` expose the pre-sigmoid step logit (a sigmoid never exceeds its cap, so LeCO's raw-above-max has no analogue) and add an optional trailing `diagnostics` field to `IntrinsicSolverOutput` (network.py:186-195, default None to keep three-way unpacking): `(z > 3).mean()`, `(z < -3).mean()`, per-cell `correction.flatten(-2).norm(-1) > 0.95` fraction, raw head norm. In `features.rms_normalize` (features.py:258) return the pre-clip `(|x| > CLIP).mean()`. In `MixedHexSolverStep.forward`'s no_grad block (mixed_physics.py:481-485) compute applied-update RMS and the achieved/requested ratio. In train_mixed.py:725-733 append the uphill count `(after > previous).sum()` and per-`MATRIX_BLOCKS` RMS plus `history_valid` mean to the all-reduced sums; add a test that `physical_axis_change` RMS > 0 after the first physical step. Extend mixed_report.py:20-44 columns; add a per-iteration increase count to the validation `relative_energy` curves (mixed_validation.py:399-419).
- Effort: small.
- Flag: none. Do not gate PlateauController on saturation (that would change the recorded plateau policy).

### 3. Per-sample backward gradient normalization at the output heads

Merged: C3 [2], L3 placement lesson [13], C1 numerics [65].

- What LeCO does: `_NormalizeBackward` (core/model.py:548-559) is identity in the forward pass; its backward divides the cotangent by its L2 norm over non-batch dims (floor 1e-12) then clamps to +/-10 (a no-op after unit normalization). It is applied to the squashed direction, after the tanh or radial squash and before the optional RMS renormalization (:2064-2070), and to `delta_log_scale` (:2116-2117), where it reduces the scale-head cotangent to sign(g). `normalize_output_gradients: true` in all three configs (hood_vto.yaml:47). Placement comment: guarding after RMS normalization "amplifies the zero-head warm start by tens of times" (analytically 1/epsilon = 100x at `direction_rms_epsilon = 1e-2`). Correction: LeCO calls the model with batch size 1 (core/sequence.py:1182-1187), so there it gives loss-magnitude invariance per call rather than equalizing samples within a batch; the multi-sample behaviour is untested in LeCO. Side effect found by LeCO's audit: under this normalization the increase-penalty weight barely changes parameter gradients (astra 2026-09-09.md:2408, 2429).
- Why it matters for LIDO: within one 16-object mixed batch (E in [1e3, 1e6], rho in [100, 1e4]; train_mixed.py:88-92) the cotangent reaching the heads scales with 1/scale (scale = max(|E_before|, floor)) and the fusion adjoint, so a few objects can dominate the Adam step. Which objects dominate is not established, hence the ablation.
- LIDO adaptation: add the Function to network.py and apply it to `raw` before `raw / sqrt(1 + |raw|^2)` (network.py:352) over dims (cells, 9) per object and to the step logit over (cells,); gate with `MixedTrainConfig.normalize_head_gradients` (default off, train_mixed.py:50). Never place it on the world increment at mixed_physics.py:463. Run finite-difference gradient checks with the flag off. Measure per-object head gradient norms on a soft/stiff two-object batch before adopting.
- Effort: small.
- Flag: none contradicted, but it re-weights the batch gradient relative to "average the losses and update Adam once" (v2-plan.md:396-398), so record it as an ablation, not a silent adoption.

### 4. Input normalization: standardize and clip the log-RMS scalar; clipping policy

Merged: C4 [3], C09 [22], N02 EMA lesson [28], C7 sequence [36], C8 numerics [69], C06-hard-clip-vs-smooth-compression [80].

- What LeCO does: in `normalization_mode: analytic` only channel 37, log RMS(|g|), goes through `RunningNormalizer` (core/model.py:983-986, "Every channel except log RMS(|g|) already has an explicit physical scale"); its statistics come from a one-time `calibrate_normalizers` pass through the untrained no-op network (train.py:204-265; 30 sequences x 4 Cells in hood_vto.yaml:137-138), then freeze; `apply_statistics` standardizes that channel and clamps all channels to +/-10 (`clip_passthrough: true`, core/model.py:485-510). The recurrent log_scale is normalized analytically and never enters the normalizer because "Including its constant warmup value in a running normalizer produced an epsilon variance and clipped every learned scale change" (core/model.py:970-972). EMA statistics exist but are dead plumbing: train.py never calls `set_normalizer_ema_accumulation` (core/model.py:114-372). LeCO floors gradient RMS at the same 1e-12 as LIDO (core/model.py:1415), so its raw log channel has the same -27.6 sentinel; the calibrated standardize-and-clip is its remedy. On clipping: in a paired ablation the clipped `body_split` model hit its scale cap in 36-96 of 200 records against 0-2 unclipped, read as the scale head compensating for clipped local shock magnitude, but the fully unclipped variant generalized worse (10.9 m drift on HOOD); LeCO kept clip=10 and recommended testing order-preserving smooth compression, which it never ran (astra 2026-08-26.md:828, 867-885, 1129-1136). Metamizer uses tanh soft clipping (astra 2026-09-09.md:1477).
- Why it matters for LIDO: `log_gradient_rms = gradient_rms.log()` is written raw into state index 59 (input_assembly.py:265); R7: a zero gradient gives ln(1e-12) = -27.6 next to +/-10-clipped blocks. R11: the previous-gradient block sits at the clip near convergence and carries mostly sign.
- LIDO adaptation: in `input_assembly.assemble_inputs` (input_assembly.py:251-266) standardize log_rms with a frozen mean/std calibrated over the first N batches of the zero-initialized network (a registered buffer on the network so it rides in `network_state`; assert std > 1e-3 at freeze) and clamp to +/-10; also clamp `inertial_axis_offset` and `physical_axis_change` (features.py:335-345) to +/-10. Bump `FEATURE_SCHEMA_VERSION` (features.py:91) because column 59 changes meaning. For clipping: add a non-default per-block compression option (10*tanh(x/10) or asinh-based) in `features.rms_normalize`, paired with the clip-fraction diagnostic of theme 2 and with hard clip=10 as the control. No EMA or running normalizer anywhere.
- Effort: small (the schema bump forces a new run).
- Flag: partial, needs Anka's decision for three sub-options. The frozen-calibration variant is inside Q8/Q10 (decision-review.md:353-360, 426-437). Raising RMS_FLOOR above 1e-12 contradicts Q10 (v2-plan.md:185); a physics-derived reference g_ref for the scalar brushes against Q8 ("Do not substitute physics-derived normalization"); smooth compression replaces the Q10 hard clip and must run as an ablation.

### 5. Magnitude channels for history

Merged: C5 [4], C6 sequence [35].

- What LeCO does: the previous update is normalized by its own RMS (core/model.py:1427), erasing its magnitude, and the previous gradient by the current RMS (:1421-1422); the only magnitude fed back is `relative_log_scale`, derived from the controller state (:1592-1600), a per-object step multiplier proxy rather than the achieved update RMS. Correction: LeCO has no log prev/current gradient RMS ratio channel and no log RMS of the previous update; the proposal below is a LIDO-specific substitute inspired by LeCO's "direction and magnitude in separate channels" principle.
- Why it matters for LIDO: `SCALAR_FEATURES = ("log_gradient_rms", "history_valid")` (features.py:69); `previous_axis_update` is own-RMS normalized (input_assembly.py:256), so the network cannot tell a 1e-5 previous move from a 0.05 one, which is the cue a grow/shrink rule needs; the two-scalar version partially substitutes for theme 1 without a controller change.
- LIDO adaptation: in `assemble_inputs` compute `log_prev_grad_ratio = clamp(log(rms_prev_grad) - log(rms_current), -10, 10)` (one extra RMS; the :255 call returns the supplied RMS) and `log_prev_update_rms = clamp(log(clamp_min(rms_prev_update, RMS_FLOOR)), -10, 10)` (axes are dimensionless, no reference needed); gate both by `history_valid` in `pack_state_features` (features.py:335-343); append to `SCALAR_FEATURES` (STATE_FEATURE_DIM 61 to 63), bump the schema, update tests/test_features.py:48. Optional, input only: store the previous per-cell `step_size` in history and feed `log(step_prev / max)`.
- Effort: small (new run required).
- Flag: none contradicted; it enlarges the Q8 input set, so record as an ablation.

### 6. Architecture variants: head-split hop groups, depth control, and branches LeCO built but left off

Merged: C6 head-split [5], L4 built-but-disabled [14], C8 multiscale lesson [62], N9 direction RMS normalization [75].

- What LeCO does: `SparseGraphAttention.forward` accepts a tuple of exact-hop relations per block; contiguous equal head groups attend over different edge sets with separate segment softmax while QKV, the edge-bias MLP (sliced per group, core/graph.py:103) and the output projection are shared, so "adding a relation changes neither the parameter count nor the Transformer-block depth" (core/graph.py:55-132; tests/test_core.py:1017-1051). Shipped configs use singleton hops [1,2,4,1]; head-split groups appear only in tests. Receptive-field evidence at 60 Hz: widened [1,2,4,8,4,2,1] passed the frozen rollout rule by 0.009 rank points with a CT regression, the radius-matched depth control [1,1,1,2,1,1,1] ranked best (2.398 vs 2.417 vs baseline 2.458), fixed 50/50 per-head multi-scale lost every pairwise, and the topology V-cycle won same-frame residual on one case but lost the 9-case rank (astra 2026-08-12.md:786-801, 2212-2375; conclusion at :2366-2370: sequential capacity matters more than long-hop edges). Pure [1,1,1,1,1] was 11-12% faster but 41%/54% worse on CT residual mean/p95 (astra 2026-08-26.md:1296-1333); the 300 Hz production config still went back to a single 1-hop block by user directive (astra 2026-09-09.md:2235-2237). Built but off in every config: direction RMS normalization (core/model.py:594-624; hood_vto.yaml:48), the zero-gated global garment token (core/graph.py:337-391; hood_vto.yaml:55; rejected on rollout metrics because the direction head inflated norms to bypass the saturated scale, astra 2026-08-05.md:1106-1114, 1276-1286), edge value messages (core/graph.py:37-53, zero-init, no test, no config), EMA statistics, V-cycle levels (core/model.py:1870-1990). No LeCO document says why; read as "not shown to help".
- Why it matters for LIDO: LIDO trains one radius-one block (`hops=(1,)`, train_mixed.py:60) on a 10x10x40 beam; gravity sag and clamped-end load transfer are beam-scale. Each extra `IntrinsicTransformerLayer` costs about 272,900 parameters (84% of the 323,214 total), so a zero-parameter head split is the cheapest receptive-field test. LIDO's edge value term is always on and not zero-initialized (network.py:89, 171-172), whereas LeCO's trained baseline is score-bias only.
- LIDO adaptation: allow tuple entries in `hops`; in `IntrinsicTransformerLayer.forward` (network.py:157-176) loop over head groups, slice q/k/v channels, gather with that group's neighbour buffers, softmax per group, concatenate before `out_projection`; keep `edge_bias` and `edge_val` shared and slice their outputs per group (network.py:88-89). Compare hops=((1,2),) vs (1,) vs (1,2) vs (1,1,1) at matched updates and wall-clock, always with a same-depth same-parameter hop-1 control, judged on the selection metric and rollout survival, never on the K=100 residual alone. Ablate `edge_val` by zero-init-and-gate, not removal. Do not try a global token or V-cycle before the hop-group test.
- Effort: medium.
- Flag: needs Anka's decision / ablation. Contradicts "one radius-one graph transformer layer per optimizer iteration ... No context jumping" (v2-plan.md:101-105; decision-review.md:165-166). Removing edge values outright would contradict the retained "learned edge attention biases and edge values"; a global token adjoins the deferred A01 question.

### 7. Ablation tooling: feature masks, mean substitution, forked-RNG construction, zero-initialized additive branches

Merged: C7 zero-init encoder branch [6], C8 RNG isolation [7], C9 feature-ablation mask [8], C16-mean-substitution-ablation-forked-rng [89].

- What LeCO does: `FEATURE_ABLATION_PRESETS` (core/model.py:16-47) multiply channel groups by a non-persistent [38] mask before the normalizer (:955-967, :1582) so every ablation has identical parameter count and checkpoint shapes; `material=False` and `physical=False` zero extra tensors outside the mask (:1205-1210, :1281-1282, :1598-1599). Optional modules are constructed under `torch.random.fork_rng(devices=[])` (core/model.py:1013-1024, 1049-1073; core/graph.py:37-47, 427-433), extra blocks reseeded from `extra_block_seed = (initial_seed + 1_000_003) % (2^63 - 1)`, so a same-seed on/off pair shares bit-identical backbone tensors (tests/test_hierarchy.py:198-236); the cross-garment proximity branch is the one exception. New input groups enter through a separate MLP with a zero-initialized last layer added to the encoder hidden state (core/model.py:1012-1023, :1673); message branches use `tanh(gate)` with gate = 0 (core/graph.py:365, 391). Before deleting pose/shape conditioning LeCO replaced it by its running mean on a trained checkpoint and saw almost no change (tshirt MAE 4.7606% to 4.7909%, astra 2026-07-23.md:1334-1337); for removed inputs it initialized the input Linear at full width and sliced columns to preserve the RNG schedule (astra models/optimizer.py:764-775). Correction: LeCO does not use the additive branch to resume a run with new inputs; `--resume` requires matching flags and `--init-checkpoint` tolerates missing keys only for listed prefixes (train.py:50-72, 787-830); the multilayer stage was a warm restart with fresh optimizer state.
- Why it matters for LIDO: notes/ablation-list.md asks to "match seeds ... and distinguish representation gains from merely increasing network capacity"; without RNG isolation a same-seed comparison confounds architecture with initialization noise. Input ablations today mean changing `STATE_FEATURE_DIM` and the schema, and every input change forces a full restart of a multi-day campaign (train_mixed.py:183-217 rejects legacy checkpoints).
- LIDO adaptation: `IntrinsicSolverNetwork(feature_ablation=...)` with a [61] non-persistent mask over `features.MATRIX_BLOCKS`, flags and scalars applied before `node_encoder` (network.py:337), presets no_history (blocks 27-44 and column 60), gradient_only, no_boundary, recorded in checkpoint metadata with a load-time mismatch error; a validation-only `--mask-block NAME` in mixed_validation.py (zero for history blocks, batch mean for flags and log_rms). Wrap construction of any optional module (FiLM at network.py:96, future extra encoders) in `fork_rng` and add a state_dict-equality test between on/off pairs (template tests/test_network.py:141-158). For new input groups add `extra_encoder` with a zero last layer and `features = node_encoder(...) + extra_encoder(extra)`; accept missing `extra_encoder.*` keys through a `_load_from_state_dict` hook (precedent hex_energy.py:321-332), framed as a warm start with fresh optimizer state.
- Effort: small each.
- Flag: none. Non-default presets switch off user-selected inputs (the current gradient is "a required input", decision-review.md:240-243), so ask before running any preset; the tooling itself is routine.

### 8. Compute savings in validation and inference: FiLM once per object, endpoint-only residual evaluation

Merged: C10 FiLM per object [9], L5 endpoint-only extra gradient [55].

### 10. Inference-only monotonic backtracking as a diagnostic

## Negative lessons (what LeCO tried and abandoned or found harmful)

Each item names its source; several exist only as code comments or logs, noted where so. Four confirmed candidates were lesson-only and live here rather than in a theme: N04 fine-tuning degradation [29], C18 step-controller antipatterns [91], C19 loss and budget antipatterns [92], C20 input antipatterns [93]; the other lessons come from the evidence text of themed candidates.

- Per-vertex step sizes: "neighboring vertices choose inconsistent step lengths ... a direct source of artificial strain" (core/model.py:2098-2101, comment only). Resetting the scale state every frame: scale reset to 1 mm then jumping to 3.9 mm within the frame, "the most direct structural stability problem" (astra 2026-07-23.md:1233).
- Widening the step bound from 2 to 3 mm made the controller saturate at 2.998 mm and worsened edge MAE to 26.7% (astra 2026-07-23.md:1310-1311); lowering `scale_rate` only delayed saturation, and one-step energy descent gives no supervision to "shrink after a reversal" (:1405-1412). A global garment token was rejected: the direction head inflated norms (0.071 to 0.191) to bypass the saturated scale, "a bypass, not a fix" (astra 2026-08-05.md:1106-1114, 1276-1286). A straight-through gradient at the scalar clamp with a componentwise direction bound reached 8.5 mm actual updates past the 5 mm nominal; the radial bound alone kept every update under 4.78 mm and was promoted (astra 2026-08-05.md:880-916). A deterministic reversal gate on the gradient cosine worked (OOD MAE 181.6% to 23.3%; removing it raised p99 residual 6.70 to 22.37 N) and was removed by user choice, not by evidence (astra 2026-07-23.md:1413-1424; 2026-07-29.md:25-45).
- Running or EMA input statistics: a constant channel during calibration gives std 1e-5 and clips every later change (core/model.py:970-972); calibration on a static start frame collapsed condition stds and invalidated 100 steps (astra 2026-07-23.md:1057-1060); statistics were polluted by committed untrained proposals (astra 2026-07-23.md:880-882); normalizer drift degraded a 60k checkpoint to 301 N until 50k buffers were swapped in (1.07 N; astra 2026-07-29.md:720). LeCO's end state is analytic scaling with one frozen-calibrated channel; the EMA code is orphaned.
- Direction RMS normalization, edge value messages, V-cycle levels: implemented, tested or not, and off in every shipped config with no recorded reason (hood_vto.yaml:48, 55; core/graph.py:37-53; core/model.py:1870-1990). Read as "not shown to help".
- Multiscale attention: the topology V-cycle won same-frame residual on one case and lost the 9-case rollout rank; fixed 50/50 per-head multi-scale lost every pairwise; depth at matched radius carried the gain (astra 2026-08-12.md:786-801, 2212-2375). All-block 6:2 head quotas stalled near 30 N residual but were trained on a build with a zeroed body-feature bug, so they are confounded (astra 2026-08-26.md:385-400, 1191-1194).
- Fixed long horizon: episode_horizons [128] against [16,32,64] accumulated 2 numerical + 1 health resets, its training-window proxies flipped sign three times, and the frozen rollout suite rejected it (penetration 4.757% vs 0.0395%, detached growth 19.84% vs 0.916%, jerk RMS 2029 vs 750; astra 2026-08-05.md:1185-1312). The exact finding is "never collapse to a single horizon", not "H = 128 is harmful"; LeCO's later window drops the shortest tiers.
- Absolute energy caps as health tests deleted finite hard samples with collision energy 1e4-1e5 (astra 2026-07-23.md:1175); a 1% bad-edge threshold reset valid states at 1.34% (astra 2026-07-29.md:149-151); with 2% and 3x, "all 17 health resets were edge-ratio; the network almost never sees recovery from stretched states" (astra 2026-08-05.md:240); a silent 10 m reset in rollout produced artifact recoveries, "never reset silently" (astra 2026-08-05.md:242).
- Monotonic acceptance: pure rejection repeated the same uphill proposal (510/700 rejected); contraction without a termination rule collapsed all environments to the scale floor (12708/14000 rejected); the straight-through clamp gradient was a modest independent improvement (0.321 to 0.226 J) that did not prevent the collapse (astra 2026-08-12.md:2633-2651; 2026-08-19.md:2-35). An inference line search masked a discontinuous false contact barrier (50.7% proposals rejected) until the objective was fixed, after which it accepted only 35.9% full steps and was slightly worse (astra 2026-08-12.md:1005-1110).
- Loss and budget: a dimensionless increase penalty added to raw tens-of-joule energies had no effect at weight 1 (astra 2026-07-23.md:1070); an early weight-1.0 penalty compressed the learned scale to 0.011-0.014 and was judged over-conservative (astra 2026-07-23.md:848), so LIDO's inherited 1.0 (train_mixed.py:64) is a config value, not a validated one; averaging the 30 inner energies rewarded early descent over end convergence (:1064-1068); relative-improvement early stopping had no "nearly lossless and cheaper" threshold (:580); a 30-Cell second-order unroll cost about 30x and was replaced by one-step on-policy training whose stability comes from long on-policy state coverage, not from backpropagating across many frames (:742-750, :1225). The asinh loss itself sits near asinh(1) = 0.881 for every update and is not a progress or selection signal (astra 2026-09-02.md:1697, 1725).
- Inputs: splitting the gradient into separately clipped body and other components broke the sum relation and caused push/pull oscillation near the solution (astra 2026-08-26.md:1239-1242); a 9-channel local Hessian block lost clearly to a bounded scalar curvature ratio eta = rho/(1+rho) (158 N vs 22 N; astra 2026-08-26.md:284, 173-177), and eta itself did not beat the no-curvature run on residual; removing input clips entirely generalized worse on HOOD (10.9 m drift) though better on CT (astra 2026-08-26.md:867-885). Absolute rest coordinates as node features are off ("absolute canonical-pose coordinate convention unique to HOOD", hood_vto.yaml:63-65; already in the one-pager).
- Fine-tuning the whole backbone on one correlated multilayer trajectory degraded the pretrained policy sharply between steps 4000 and 5000 (astra 2026-08-12.md:2597); the branch-only follow-up stalled because the exactly-zero gate was the only branch tensor with a gradient at init (astra 2026-08-12.md:2649); the 10x gate-LR recipe was never concluded and was later removed (astra commit 8432d8f). Lesson for any LIDO domain extension: freeze the backbone, train zero-gated branches, and compare against the untouched checkpoint under the new physics.
- A direction-only preconditioned update alpha*p with p = -dt^2 g/m failed at 60 Hz where elastic curvature dominates inertia; a bounded hybrid helped 60 Hz but never achieved cross-dt transfer (astra 2026-08-12.md:1986-2100; branch never merged).
- State noise at reset (3 mm, later 10% of median rest edge) was deleted in a wholesale revert with no recorded rationale; the only documented negative is that 3 mm noise amplified edge-gate resets on dense meshes (astra 2026-07-29.md:131-135).
- Tooling drift: a deleted frame-grid check and a hard-coded "30 Cells" title left two comparison scripts disagreeing about what a valid comparison is (LeCO commit e1c7d17; scripts/plot_diagnostic_comparison.py:198-200).
