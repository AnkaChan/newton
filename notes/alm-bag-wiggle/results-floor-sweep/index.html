Does lowering rho make ALM elasticity converge faster?

The current 9k floor changes the measured force residual only modestly. A k floor is a stronger candidate to investigate; much smaller rho can leave the material stress far behind the current deformation.

Why rho matters here
For a quadratic material row with stiffness k, eliminating its auxiliary strain gives an effective scalar curvature and a transmitted stress:
k_eff = k rho / (k + rho)
t = k_eff C + [k / (k + rho)] y
y_next = t
At a 9k floor, k_eff = 0.9k: the row retains 90% of its stiffness. Floors of k, 0.1k and 0.01k retain 50%, 9.09% and 0.99%, respectively, when the floor dominates the inertia metric. This explains why the current implementation changes the solve only modestly.
Lower rho also slows how quickly the stress history catches up. Holding C fixed, the constitutive mismatch contracts by k / (k + rho) per dual update: 0.1 at 9k, 0.5 at k, 0.909 at 0.1k and 0.990 at 0.01k. Inertia-only triangle rho can be orders of magnitude smaller than k. Its ALM residual may be small while the original residual remains large.
There is a second limitation: triangle stretching is represented by the scalar invariant C = ||F||, plus the area row. Its transverse curvature contains t / ||F||, which returns to the original shear stiffness at constitutive consistency. Lowering the scalar-row rho does not remove every stiff deformation mode.
A lower one-substep residual does not establish a safe new default. This sweep resets histories, samples only two trajectory states per stiffness and changes triangle and bending floors together. The report leaves the production 9k policy unchanged.


What was measured, and what was controlled
r_i = f_original_elastic,i + f_damping,i + f_contact,i + m_i (x_hat_i − x_i) / dt²
RMS = sqrt( sum over free vertices ||r_i||² / N_free )
Elasticity, material damping and inertia are evaluated in float64 at the solver’s float32 accepted positions. Contact forces are freshly evaluated using native float32 particle–rigid and cloth self-contact kernels, the current rigid poses and the native contact parameters. Cached per-color forces are not reused. Gravity and the external predictor force enter through x_hat. Pinned vertices are excluded.
  • The bag has 1,657 particles, 3,240 triangles, 4,896 edges and 72 pinned rim vertices, with three rigid objects inside. Triangle ke ranges from 1e3 through 1e7; ka = 0.2 ke. Bending ke stays 200. Material damping is unchanged.
  • An ALM-off trajectory uses the original 60 fps, 10 substeps per frame, 10 iterations per substep, 1 s settling and prescribed rim wiggle. Complete states are sampled at frame 60 and frame 180. Each candidate receives the next frame’s pin motion and runs only the first substep.
  • Every candidate uses a fresh solver, a fresh initial collision query and the same full state. Elastic ALM starts from constitutive stress seeds at the incoming pose; rigid contact history is cold. Native contact refresh, rigid updates and DAT scheduling remain active. This is not the proposed, unimplemented ALM contact design.
  • Rho is frozen during the measured substep: rho = max(rho_inertia, floor × k). Both triangle rows and dihedral bending receive the same floor factor. The inertial formulas, clamping and rho_scale = 1 are otherwise unchanged.
  • The measured residual covers particle force balance. It excludes rigid-body residuals and DAT constraint reaction forces; it is not a full coupled KKT residual or a guarantee of collision feasibility. Native contact penalty parameters can evolve during the solve.
  • No wall-clock speed comparison is made: extra force evaluations, float64 arithmetic and readback instrumentation distort timing. This also does not replace a retained-history, many-step trajectory comparison.


Validation and reproducibility
The diagnostic force matched an independently differentiated NumPy energy to relative error 5.49e-09 and the native float32 elastic-plus-damping force to 1.56e-07 on a deformed two-triangle, one-hinge fixture. ALM-off and 9k instrumented solves were separately compared against plain native solves; the largest coordinate difference was 2.98e-08 m. All 60 cases were finite, each has 101 samples, and all six modes in every pair have matching initial full-state hashes and original residuals.
