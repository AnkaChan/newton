NEWTON · LEARNED INTRINSIC DEFORMATION OPTIMIZER

From a grid of cells
to a learned solver step.

How the implemented network reads a deformed object together with the gradient of its physical energy, proposes local axis changes, assembles one shared shape, and learns from the energy of the result.

This walkthrough follows train_mixed.run_training() and MixedHexSolverStep, using the saved campaign configuration. One physical timestep advances simulation time by 1/300 s. Within it, several solver iterations improve a candidate shape while the timestep's physical reference (the inertial prediction and the starting positions) stays fixed. One network query performs one such iteration: it reads the candidate, proposes a change for every cell, and the trainer scores the assembled result by its physical energy.

The graph nodes are cells: a 10 × 10 × 40 grid of cubic hexahedra with 0.025 m edges. The actual simulated shape is stored as shared corner positions. Each cell describes itself by one 3 × 3 deformation gradient at its center, expressed in its own rotation frame. A local proposal from one cell cannot independently move a corner that is also used by neighboring cells; a sparse least-squares assembly reconciles the proposals into one compatible shape, and the prescribed corners of the clamped face are enforced exactly.

Every query also receives the direction of steepest energy descent. The gradient of the implicit-Euler objective with respect to the corner positions is computed by autograd at the candidate, pulled back through the transpose of the assembly solve into a per-cell 3 × 3 axis gradient, normalized by its per-object RMS, and packed into the node input next to the geometry. Together with the previous query's gradient and achieved update, this turns the network into a learned optimizer step rather than a shape predictor.

Scope of this snapshot. The physical energy uses Newton's stable Neo-Hookean law, a polynomial in the deformation gradient that stays finite through collapse and inversion; no Gauss point is rejected for its Jacobian sign, and only nonfinite values raise. Cell frames are the closest proper rotations of the center deformation, with a deterministic tie-break built from three prescribed corners of the clamped face. The node input holds six nine-value blocks in that frame (the current local axes, the inertial offset, the physical axis change, the projected energy gradient, the previous query's gradient and its achieved update), fourteen boundary flags, the log gradient RMS and a history flag: 70 values per cell. The network emits a bounded nine-value correction and one sigmoid step per cell in (0, 0.05). The assembled shape is evaluated exactly as proposed: there is no geometry backtracking, acceptance scaling or step shortening.

CPU preparation (the rigid initializer and the candidate draw) happens outside the learned update. Neural operations, the energy and its position gradient run on the GPU. The assembly solve and its transpose run on a cached CPU sparse factor; the transpose serves both the backward pass and the gradient input of the next query.

A small vocabulary for the rest of the page
Cell / hexahedron
A volume element with eight corner references. Rest cells are cubes; current corners can form a warped, collapsed or inverted shape.
Deformation gradient F
A 3 × 3 matrix describing how a small displacement in rest coordinates changes in the current shape. Here it is evaluated once per cell at the cell center; its columns are the three transformed material axes.
Frame R
The proper rotation closest to F in the Frobenius norm, computed by SVD and held fixed for one query. It is the coordinate system in which a cell reads and writes its vectors.
Local axes A
The deformation axes expressed in R: A = RᵀF. The axes retain length and angle changes, and R A = F holds exactly, also for inverted cells.
Axis gradient
The derivative of the physical energy with respect to a cell's world axis increment, obtained by pulling the corner-position gradient back through the transpose of the assembly solve. Expressed in R and divided by its per-object RMS, it is the network's descent-direction input.
Optimizer history
Two detached 3 × 3 blocks per cell carried from the previous learned query: its un-normalized world axis gradient and the change of F that its assembled update produced. Both are stored in world coordinates, re-expressed in the current frame when read, and accompanied by a history_valid flag.
Energy floor
A per-object scale, 2⁻²³ × rest volume × (λ + 2μ + η/dt + ρh²/dt²) joules, below which an energy counts as numerically zero. The loss divides by max(|E_before|, floor).
Gauss point
A sample location inside an element used to approximate its energy integral. There are eight per cell here.
Gradient / backward pass
A gradient measures how a scalar changes when its inputs change. Backpropagation combines these derivatives to update the network weights.
Detach / no_grad
A deliberate boundary that prevents differentiation through an earlier computation or state. Frames, the gradient input and the history are detached; local axes, assembly and energy are not.
Factorization / adjoint
A factorization prepares a fixed matrix for repeated linear solves. The adjoint is the transposed solve used both to backpropagate through assembly and to form the axis gradient input.
