## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Where may the blade cut? | Anywhere the blade goes. The cut follows existing tet faces; vertices on the cut get copies from pre-allocated spare particles (option 1 of `options.md`). | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Anywhere the blade goes" over "Only on a sheet planned in the mesh" and "Planned sheet first, free cut second". Same choice as in the first run. |
| D2 | How do the two wound faces start, given that a pair at distance zero gets no force and no truncation plane? | A position offset. At the split, the original and its copy each move by half a start gap into their own material. No kernel changes. Default start gap = the self-contact margin. Smaller gaps (half, a tenth of the margin) are ablation arms. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Move the two particles apart" over "Move them apart in the rest shape too" and "New contact rule for cut pairs". The rest-shape form stays an ablation arm (fork F6 of `options.md`). The new contact rule is not pursued. Open detail for `design.md`: the direction rule on a stepped cut, and what happens to a vertex with no safe direction. |
| D3 | What must the first implementation stage show? | A flat through cut, then a stitch. One block held at both ends by grippers; the blade on a grid plane cuts through the whole cross-section; the grippers pull the halves 4 mm apart; three stitches; one gripper lets go. The pass tests are on this scene. A stepped cut and a partial cut each get a small test of their own (cut and contact only, no stitch). The design itself covers the free cut in every case. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Flat through cut, then stitch" over "Flat through cut plus a tilted cut with stitch" and "Partial cut with stitch". |
| D4 | Does the blade touch the tissue? | Yes. The blade has contact with the tissue, and a contact force starts the cut, like the suture puncture. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Contact, and a force starts the cut" against my recommendation "No contact in stage 1". This replaces forks F3 and F4 of `options.md` draft 2 (armed plus geometry; no blade contact). Open after this decision: how the cut goes on after its start, which force is measured, and how the blade is driven. |
| D5 | After the force has started the cut, how does the cut go on? | The wound opens ahead of the blade edge, and the blade has no gate. The blade keeps full contact with the tissue at all times. Each time the edge force passes the cut force, the faces at the pressed vertices that the blade plane separates are cut (one tet row). The blade wedges into the slot, reaches the bottom of the wound and presses again. The blade never enters uncut tissue. The blade edge is round (a capsule). | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "The wound opens ahead of the edge, no gate" over "Like the needle: a gate after the start" and "Force at the start, then no blade contact". Consequences: no change in the suture gate code; the cut leads the edge by up to one tet; the rule of part 1.4 of `options.md` draft 2 (blade plate between two centroids) is replaced. Not run yet. |
| D6 | Which force is compared with the cut force of the tissue? | The total force on the blade edge. One kernel sums the normal contact force of the records of the blade edge shape, taken along the cutting direction. The sum is compared with a per-particle tissue value `vbd.cut_force` [N] (new), read at the vertex with the strongest record. This is the suture puncture rule with a new attribute. An armed switch cuts at the first contact, for tests. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Total force on the edge" over "Force per length of contact" and "Force at each vertex on its own". Known price: the value depends on the contact length. Force per length stays a fork. |
| D7 | How is the blade driven? | The blade is a dynamic rigid body, held by a kinematic holder through a joint with drives, as the suture needle. It moves in every substep, the tissue can hold it back, and the joint carries the cutting force to the holder. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Dynamic blade on a kinematic holder" over "Kinematic blade" and "Both, as a scene choice". |
| D8 | Where do the cut update and the BVH rebuild run? | The cut kernels are the last call of each `SolverVBD.step`, on the device, inside the captured graph. The captured frame starts with `rebuild_bvh`, in every frame, as four shipped examples do. No host logic and no readback. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Cut in the step, rebuild in every captured frame" over "rebuild on the host after a cut" and "refit only". This replaces the first-run statement "BVH rebuild on the host between graph replays". Open: the cost of one rebuild for the tissue size is not measured. |
| D9 | Which color does a copy get? | A second color group per original color (a copy color). A copy of a vertex with color c comes from the spare pool of the copy color of c. No color array changes at run time. A switch keeps the inherited color as an ablation arm. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "A second color group per original color" over "The same, and empty copy colors are skipped" and "The color of the original". Known price: the number of color passes doubles from the first frame. Skipping empty copy colors with `wp.capture_if` stays a fork. |
| D10 | How do unused spares stay out of contact? | They are parked away from everything. Unused spares sit on a parking lattice: places more than one query radius apart, away from the tissue and from every tool path, inside the scene bounds. An unused triangle row holds one parking particle three times. No kernel changes. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Park them away from everything" over "Skip unused rows in the detection kernels" and "Parking now, skip rule as a measured fork". Known price: nothing enforces the keep-out zone; a host check at construction tests the start state only. |
| D11 | Ten smaller defaults, asked as one list | All ten accepted as defaults; each stays a switch with an ablation entry or a stated limit in `design.md`. (a) Topological contact filter threshold 1 and rest-shape exclusion 0 on a cut body. (b) Cut scenes set `particle_edge_parallel_epsilon` for the mesh scale (1e-7 or less). (c) Wound faces get edge rows, one per fan of tets around a wound edge, so edge-edge contact acts. (d) A copy takes the share of the mass of its original that fits the rest volume of its tets. (e) A pinned vertex, or one named by an attachment, is not split and stays shared. (f) When a spare pool is empty the vertex stays shared, a device counter grows, the host reads it after the cut; no error inside the step. (g) Spares are handed out by atomic counters; the solver raises an error at construction in a Warp deterministic mode. (h) One line in the suture puncture kernel skips the tet with zero stiffness that unused adjacency records point at; this is the only change in existing kernel code. (i) `reset` restores the edited arrays from arrays saved at construction. (j) Stage-1 limits: box and capsule tools, zero stiffness of skin triangles and edges, one world, no cut after the first puncture. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal: "Accept all ten". |
| D12 | May host-only checks run before `design.md` is written? | Yes, all three: (1) color count of the stage-1 grid from `builder.color()`; (2) replay of the chosen cut rule on the stage-1 mesh for the blade on a grid plane, a quarter and half a cell off, and tilted 3, 10 and 15 degrees; (3) start gap direction on the same cases and on one unstructured tissue mesh. No solver, no stepping, no GPU. Results go into `design.md` marked as host-only checks; scripts stay in the task folder. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal: "Run all three now". Checks that need the solver wait for the implementation and for her word. |
| D13 | Form and place of `design.md` | Chapters in the form of the suture design (eight fixed sections each), in the task folder, and also a design pull request: the document is committed under `notes/` on a dev branch of the design worktree, pushed to AnkaChan/newton, and a pull request is opened against a feature branch for line-by-line review. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal: "Chapters, and a design pull request". This is her OK for that one push and that one pull request. No other GitHub action is covered. |
| D14 | After the first tet row the wound is a V, and the two wound faces squeeze the blade edge. By a hand estimate this squeeze gives about 1 N along the cutting direction before the edge is near the cut front, so from the second row on a row is cut when a cut-front vertex comes within the contact range, and the cut leads the edge by about 5 mm. How does the design treat this? | Keep it, and measure it first. The design stays as written: every contact record of the blade edge counts. The behavior is stated as a limit and as a fork (F2.5, F2.6). Before the implementation one small run (P0) measures it: a block with the top tet row already split and one start gap open, a capsule lowered into it on a holder drive, the contact force read per record. No cut code. | Anka, 2026-11-10, terminal. She chose "Keep it, and measure it first" over "Keep it, no run now", "Only the cut front counts" and "Smaller contact range". This is her word for the one run P0 in the design worktree. |

| O1 | How does the cut go on below the first tet row? (fork F3.4 of `design.md`) | Open. Run P0 (D14) measured that the blade edge rests on the two wound lips and that no cut-front vertex comes within the contact range (smallest distance 3.29 mm).
