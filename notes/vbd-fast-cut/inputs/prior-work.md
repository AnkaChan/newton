## 1. The suture feature

**1.1 What it does.** A needle (a kinematic rigid body) and a thread (capsule bodies) pass through tetrahedral tissue. The tissue mesh does not change. The path is a tunnel: a list of tunnel points, each stored as one tet index plus four barycentric weights. Each point carries a constraint row that holds the needle or the thread on the path. The row adds force and Hessian to the four corners of its tet. Sources: `suture_kernels.py:96-101`, `suture_kernels.py:130-137`, `tasks/vbd-suture/CONTEXT.md:7-21`.

**1.2 Pre-allocation.** The user gives two capacities per needle in the builder call `add_suture_needle`: `max_tunnels` (default 4) and `max_points_per_tunnel` (default 256). The solver constructor allocates every suture array once with N x T x Q rows. A free slot holds -1. Slots are never reused. A full needle stops making tunnels without an error; only point overflow has a device flag. Sources: `vbd-suture/newton/_src/sim/builder.py:11372-11381`, `vbd-suture/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:3458-3461`, `suture_kernels.py:709-716`.

**1.3 Hooks in the step.** The suture code runs inside `SolverVBD.step`. After the particle initialization it refreshes the row caches. In each iteration it accumulates the row forces once per particle color, before the elasticity solve of that color. After the last iteration, one kernel with one thread per needle runs the state machine (puncture, a tet walk bounded by 32 steps, point creation, exit), and one kernel with one thread per particle recomputes the contact gate. Sources: `vbd-suture/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:3042-3066`, `suture_kernels.py:833`, `suture_kernels.py:605-606`.

**1.4 How it stays graph-capturable.** Five mechanisms: all arrays exist before capture; all launch sizes are host constants; unused slots return early; a device counter and a device "due" flag replace host branches; counters and the overflow flag stay on the device. The step path has no host readback. A test captures one step, replays it and compares with a plain run. The example captures one frame of 10 substeps. Between two graph launches the example only writes array contents on the host (kinematic poses, attachment switches). Sources: `vbd-suture/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:3065-3066`, `vbd-suture/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:3484-3491`, `test_suture_needle_state.py:298-313`, `examples/vbd/example_vbd_suture.py:622-627`, `examples/vbd/example_vbd_suture.py:629-637`.

**1.5 What it assumes about the tissue.** The suture design states that the tet mesh keeps its topology. Four data sets depend on that. (a) A private table of tet face neighbors, int32 [tet_count, 4], built once on the host in the solver constructor. The tet walk leaves the tissue only through a face whose entry is negative. Surface triangles play no part. (b) The model's vertex-to-tet table: the puncture searches the tets of the contact particle, so a particle with an empty row can never start a tunnel. (c) The full vertex adjacency, read once per step for the row metric. (d) The particle colors: a row scatters to the tet corners of the current color, and the design note relies on four different colors per tet. The kernels read `model.tet_indices` live, so a tunnel point follows a corner that is re-pointed to another particle. The kernels hold no body id and no particle range. Five per-particle attributes (`vbd.suture_grip`, `suture_mu`, `suture_puncture_force`, `suture_puncture_cos`, `suture_stiffness`) are read through the tet corners. No suture kernel writes `tet_indices` or a particle array other than the gate, the forces and the Hessians. Sources: `tasks/vbd-suture/CONTEXT.md:11-13`, `vbd-suture/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:3532-3537`, `suture_kernels.py:50-75`, `suture_kernels.py:908-911`, `suture_kernels.py:856-876`, `suture_kernels.py:490-495`, `study/verify-suture-claims.md`.

**1.6 The contact gate.** Two bit masks switch needle and thread contact with tissue off near a tunnel: a static bit per shape and a dynamic bit per particle. The particle bits come from a distance test only: no side test, no topology test. A rigid-soft record whose shape and particle share a bit is still detected, but it gives no force and no truncation plane, and the puncture test ignores it. The gate radius is fixed at solver construction; it is 2.9 mm with the demo values. The gate does not act on tissue self contact. It is not a general interface: only `add_suture_needle` sets shape bits, and the solver rewrites all particle bits at each suture update. Upstream main has no gate. Anka chose this form over a filter at detection. Inference: on a closed cut, the particles of the second face within 2.9 mm of the exit point of the first tunnel are gated, so the needle cannot puncture the second face there. The demo never met this case, because its wound starts 4 mm open. Sources: `vbd-suture/newton/_src/sim/builder.py:11476-11478`, `suture_kernels.py:1029-1066`, `vbd-suture/newton/_src/geometry/soft_contacts_mesh.py:1612-1621`, `tasks/vbd-suture/docs/adr/0002-contact-gate-at-consumption.md:8`, `study/verify-suture-claims.md`.

**1.7 The demo.** The wound is two separate blocks (two `add_soft_grid` calls, 14 x 10 x 4 cells of 3 mm) with a gap of 4 mm. No element is cut. Settings: 60 frames per second, 10 substeps, 20 iterations, E = 1e5 Pa, particle radius 0.5 mm, self-contact margin 1 mm and gap 1 mm, contact stiffness 5e2 N/m, rigid-soft truncation on, full-surface rigid-soft contact on. All rigid shapes are boxes and capsules. Closed wound faces rest about 1 mm apart, which is the self-contact margin. The two blocks have disjoint color sets, built in the example from the particle range of each block. Sources: `examples/vbd/example_vbd_suture.py:359-378`, `examples/vbd/example_vbd_suture.py:49-66`, `examples/vbd/example_vbd_suture.py:257-263`, `tasks/vbd-suture/use-guide-2026-11-09-suture-demo.md:129-130`.

**1.8 Lessons in its design record.** (a) Two faces in self contact jittered while their vertices shared colors. Softer contact, a smaller gap and 60 iterations did not help. Disjoint color sets cut the face motion about ten times, at about 50 percent more time per iteration (decision I31). (b) Per-particle contact misses a shape that is thinner than the vertex spacing. A 0.3 mm thread sank through a block with particles 3 mm apart. Full-surface contact fixed it (I17). (c) In the suture grill Anka reversed three recommendations: the gate moved from detection to consumption, the state machine from the host to the device, and the code from a companion class into `SolverVBD`. (d) The plan to combine the suture with the earlier cut work was a proposal and never ran. It names the target demo: cut, pull the halves apart a little, stitch, release. Sources: `tasks/vbd-suture/decisions.md:57`, `tasks/vbd-suture/decisions.md:43`, `tasks/vbd-suture/options.md:17-26`, `tasks/vbd-suture/plan-2026-11-06-cut-and-suture.md:107-113`.

## 2. What VBD offers (upstream main)

**2.1 Elasticity reads the model live.** `SolverVBD` keeps no copy of tet indices, rest matrices, materials, masses or flags. Every launch passes the model arrays. The rest state of a tet is one 3 x 3 matrix; the rest volume comes from its determinant in the kernel. Tets store no density. The particle path keeps no solver history between steps. `notify_model_changed` has no branch for particles, tets, triangles, colors or adjacency. No Newton code writes a model index array on the device today, and no rule allows or forbids it. Inference: a kernel can edit these arrays in place between two steps, and the next step uses the new values. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:4517-4541`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/sim/builder.py:11753-11761`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:3116-3124`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:1620-1657`, `study/verify-graph-capture.md`.

**2.2 Vertex adjacency is tight and trusted.** The solve finds the tets of a vertex in a CSR table with one (tet, local corner) record per incidence. The table has no validity flag and no spare room. The kernel does not check that the tet corner still holds the vertex: a stale record gives the vertex the force of another particle, with no error. For a record whose tet has k_mu = 0 and k_lambda = 0, both elasticity kernels read only the two material values and skip the rest. The table is built once at `finalize`, and one device struct is shared by the solver and the rigid-soft contact code. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/utils/mesh.py:280-298`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/particle_vbd_kernels.py:1999-2016`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:1232-1236`, `study/verify-adjacency-elasticity.md`.

**2.3 Colors are host-static.** `particle_color_groups` is a Python list with one device array per color. The solve loops over the list in Python, and the launch size comes from the size of each group. A captured graph therefore fixes the number of colors and each group size. Only the contents are device data. A second array, `particle_colors`, holds one color per particle; the contact kernels read it, and the two must agree. `set_coloring` checks nothing. A slot must hold a valid particle index; a slot with an inactive particle does nothing; a particle must not be listed twice. `builder.color()` puts a particle without elements into color 0. The coloring graph holds elements with non-zero stiffness only; contact and springs are not part of it. Per iteration and per color the solver runs two full self-contact passes, one truncation pass over all particles and the solve, so each extra color costs these passes. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:4333-4334`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/sim/builder.py:13312-13318`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:4454-4458`, `study/verify-coloring-launch.md`.

**2.4 Inert entities.** A tet with k_mu = 0 and k_lambda = 0 adds nothing to the solve. A particle without the ACTIVE flag, or with zero mass, is not integrated and not moved by the solve; the forward step tests the inverse mass and the solve tests the mass, so an edit must write both. Self contact does not read particle flags: a parked particle is a fixed point obstacle. A triangle row with three equal indices acts as that point; an edge row with equal end indices too. The builder drops a triangle with zero area, so such a row must be written into the index array directly. With full-surface rigid-soft contact, the edge and face records of a box or capsule also ignore the ACTIVE flag. Inference: an unused spare must stay farther than the query radius from all live geometry, from every rigid shape, and from other parked particles. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/particle_vbd_kernels.py:2004`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/particle_vbd_kernels.py:1488-1492`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/geometry/tri_mesh_collision.py:947-978`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/sim/builder.py:11569-11571`, `study/verify-spare-primitives.md`, `study/verify-full-surface-contact.md`.

**2.5 Self contact: data.** A tet body has triangles and edges only on its surface. They have zero stiffness and exist for collision. Interior faces are stored nowhere, and no tet-neighbor table exists in upstream main. The detector reads `tri_indices` and `edge_indices` live at every refit, detection, force and truncation launch. Fixed at construction: the BVH item counts, the result buffers (32 pairs per vertex, 64 per edge by default), the world group of each row, and the launch sizes. On overflow a pair is dropped; a flag is set and nothing reads it. A pair at distance zero still uses a buffer slot. Inference: a spare triangle row that exists at construction has a BVH leaf and buffer rows, and an in-place write of its indices makes it collide. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/sim/builder.py:12495-12504`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/geometry/tri_mesh_collision.py:905-911`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/geometry/tri_mesh_collision.py:451-467`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/geometry/kernels.py:1664-1670`.

**2.6 Self contact: filters.** Detection skips a pair that shares a vertex. This test reads the live index arrays. The n-ring filter lists are different: the detector builds them once on the host, from the surface edges of the uncut mesh, when the threshold is 2 or more. The solver default is 2. The rows are sorted, exact-size device arrays. An interior particle and a spare particle have an empty row. With threshold 1 no list exists; a shipped tet example uses threshold 1. The rest-shape exclusion (default radius 0, off) measures distance in `model.particle_q`. Inference: after a cut, a stale list still hides old skin triangles in the second ring across the cut, and it knows nothing of the new cut faces. Any positive rest-shape radius would remove exactly the pairs between the two cut faces, because the faces coincide in the rest shape. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/geometry/kernels.py:1618-1639`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/geometry/tri_mesh_collision.py:786-819`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/examples/multiphysics/example_softbody_gift.py:224-227`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/geometry/kernels.py:1650-1660`, `study/verify-contact-filters.md`.

**2.7 Self contact: zero distance.** The contact is unsigned. The force normal is the direction from the closest point to the vertex. No part of detection, force or truncation knows an inside or an outside. A pair at distance exactly 0 gets no force. The truncation builds a plane only for a strictly positive gap; a touching pair is not constrained. A vertex that has already passed through a face gets a normal plane on the wrong side, and the force pushes it further in: a crossed state is stable. A gap between about 2 and 20 micrometres freezes an approaching vertex. With the demo values (stiffness 5e2 N/m, margin 1 mm) one pair pushes with 0.25 N at 0.5 mm, 1.25 N at 0.1 mm and 25 N just above zero. For a start gap of 0.1 mm or more with each vertex on its correct side, the truncation builds planes that stop the faces from crossing. Every particle is also capped at 0.5 x relaxation x (margin + gap) of motion per detection, about 1 m/s with the demo values. Inference: new faces need a positive start gap with every vertex on its correct side, or a new rule for pairs at distance zero. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/particle_vbd_kernels.py:1293-1299`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/particle_vbd_kernels.py:2455-2456`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/particle_vbd_kernels.py:866-882`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/rigid_vbd_kernels.py:7746-7751`, `study/verify-zero-gap-contact.md`.

**2.8 Self contact: short edges.** The edge-edge code passes `particle_edge_parallel_epsilon` (default 1e-5) to Warp, and Warp compares it with the squared edge length. An edge of 3.16 mm or less then counts as its first end point, in detection and in the force. The suture demo has 3 mm cells, so its axis-aligned edges are in this class. Inference: a scene with millimetre elements must set this value, or edge-edge contact acts between end points only. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/geometry/kernels.py:1797`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:458`, Warp 1.19.0 `warp/native/intersect.h` lines 1138-1155, `study/verify-spare-primitives.md`.

**2.9 Step order and graph capture.** The particle path is: detection, forward step, truncation, second detection, then per iteration and per color: contact accumulation, elasticity solve, truncation; then the velocity update. The path has no host readback and no allocation. A captured graph fixes array pointers, shapes, launch sizes and every Python branch. Array contents stay free: tests edit a device count and a state array between replays, and the replay reads the new content. No test edits an index array, an adjacency array or a color group between replays. Four SolverVBD examples call `rebuild_bvh` once per frame inside the captured function. One Newton class already gates launches on a device flag with `wp.capture_if`. Sources: `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py:2932-2935`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/tests/test_solver_vbd.py:2739-2793`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/examples/cloth/example_cloth_twist.py:240-243`, `vbd-fast-cut-design/newton/_src/sim/collision_schedule.py:152-153`, `study/verify-graph-capture.md`.

**2.10 Warp tools.** No launch can take its size from device data; the pattern is a fixed maximum size plus an early return. A launch of size zero is dropped from the graph. `wp.capture_if` gates a group of launches on a one-element device flag. It needs CUDA 12.4 or newer; this machine has driver 570 with CUDA 12.8. The gated body must not allocate. `wp.Bvh.rebuild` works in place with a fixed item count and is capture-safe at the top level of a graph. It allocates nine temporary buffers per call, so, by inference, it raises an error inside a gated body. `Bvh.refit` does not allocate. `wp.atomic_add` returns the old value, which gives unique pool slots without a readback; the slot order is not reproducible. All of this exists in Warp 1.17, the Newton minimum. Warp 1.18 ships `delaunay_edge_flip`, an in-place, capturable mesh edit built on atomic claims. **2.11 Rigid-soft contact and a blade.** By default a rigid shape touches tissue particles only, so a thin blade passes between particles. Full-surface contact (faces and edges) is an option of the collision pipeline. Its candidate lists hold row ids of all triangle and edge rows that exist at construction. The records store particle ids, read from the live index arrays at detection time. Rigid-soft contact does not use the winding of a soft triangle. ## 3. XFEM theory

**3.1 One space, several forms.** Classic XFEM keeps the mesh and adds unknowns to the nodes of a cut element (a jump function, and special functions at the crack tip). The phantom-node method and the virtual node algorithm instead copy the cut element. Each copy owns the material on one side, and the nodes on the far side are replaced by new nodes. For a full cut all three span the same displacements. They differ in integration and mass. The copy form has ordinary elements and ordinary nodes, so every unknown stays a particle position. Sources: Koschier, Bender, Thuerey 2017, section 2; Song, Menouillard, Tabarraei 2013, section 3; Molino, Bao, Fedkiw 2004, section 3. Belytschko and Black 1999, Moes et al. 1999, Hansbo and Hansbo 2004 and Song et al. 2006 were read as abstracts only.

**3.2 What changes per cut.** One extra element per cut element, new nodes for the far side, and a rewrite of node indices. Molino copies the full element with full mass, on purpose, for stability; this adds mass and stiffness. The XFEM side scales each copy by its material share. Inference: for a linear tet the share is one number per copy and is exact. Sources: Molino et al. 2004, section 3; Koschier et al. 2017, sections 2 and 7; Jerabkova et al. 2006, section 2.

**3.3 Known problems.** A node with a very small material share makes an implicit system ill-conditioned, up to singular. Koschier perturbs the cut, constrains such unknowns and rescales. The earlier cut work met the same effect as masses down to a fraction of 5.64e-14. A cut whose tip ends inside an element does not open in any of these methods: the opening advances element by element. Robust cut registration needed double precision in Wang et al. 2014. Koschier's method took 1.375 s per step for 18.5k tets on a laptop CPU. Sources: Koschier et al. 2017, figure 14 and section 7; `vbd-xfem-surgical-cut/contact-color-majorization-review.md:73-75`; Sifakis, Der, Fedkiw 2007, section 5; Wang, Jiang, Schroeder, Teran 2014, section 1.

**3.4 The cut surface and contact.** In every method of this family the cut faces are an embedded surface inside the elements. They are not element faces and their vertices are not nodes. The survey says the same: only element duplication needs a separate surface mesh. Few papers collide the cut faces. Molino collides embedded nodes and maps the correction to the parent nodes; the new faces can touch right after the split, and no collision-free start is guaranteed for volumes. Sifakis resolves collisions on a soft constrained copy of the surface and points to a second paper for the binding. Koschier 2017 has no collision handling at all. Sifakis shows an incision that springs close again. Sources: Molino et al. 2004, section 6; Sifakis et al. 2007, section 4 and figure 10; Sifakis, Shinar, Irving, Fedkiw 2007, section 3; Koschier et al. 2017, section 7; Wu, Westermann, Dick 2014, figure 1.

**3.5 Fit to VBD.** Inference: the copy form fits fixed arrays (spare nodes, spare elements, index rewrites), and a new node can keep the color of its parent. The hard part is the embedded surface. VBD self contact works on particles and on triangles of particles. An embedded surface needs either a new contact path on barycentric points or extra surface particles with stiff ties. The earlier cut work took the first route and got three index spaces, which broke the suture kernels. Overlapping full-shape copies also break the suture tet walk, which tests whole tets. Sources for the last two sentences: `vbd-xfem-surgical-cut/integration-notes.md:106-112`.

## 4. Methods outside XFEM

**4.1 Survey.** Wu, Westermann and Dick sort tetrahedral cutting into six techniques: element deletion, splitting along existing faces, element duplication, vertex snapping, element refinement, and snapping plus refinement. Their verdict: deletion and face splitting keep well-shaped elements but give a jagged surface; snapping and refinement follow the tool but must prevent bad elements. The survey calls the physical contact response in cutting an open question. Source: Wu, Westermann, Dick 2014 (Eurographics report, preprint), sections 2.2 and 7.

**4.2 Splitting along existing faces.** A node gets one copy per face-connected group of elements around it. Only node indices change. Elements, rest data and the element count stay as they are. The Vertex Block Descent paper names this case: copies inherit the color of the original, and no re-coloring is needed. Its example is cloth tearing on the CPU; it has no tet cut. The cut lags the tool by up to one element, and the surface is jagged unless the mesh was built to contain the cut surface. Two papers state the mass rule: each node gets one quarter of the mass of each of its own tets. A shipped game engine used this method, rejected element splitting because the element count cannot be planned, and capped the fracture events per step. Sources: Nienhuys and van der Stappen 2001, sections 2.4 and 3.5; Mueller and Gross 2004, section 4; Chen, Liu, Yang, Yuksel 2024, section 3.9; Steinemann, Harders, Gross, Szekely 2006, section 4.7; Parker and O'Brien 2009, section 5.

**4.3 Interfaces that exist before the run.** Cohesive and discontinuous Galerkin meshes give each element its own nodes and bond the elements. A cut changes only a bond state. On all faces this costs about 15 to 18 times more vertices (my arithmetic from two published meshes) and changes the material response before any cut. DiSECt uses one planned cut surface on the GPU: it duplicates the cut elements once before the run, joins the two sides with springs, and lets the knife weaken the springs. The cut place is fixed before the run. The paper has no contact between the two cut sides, and it is not real time (30 s of compute per simulated second). Sources: Radovitzky et al. 2011, introduction; Heiden et al. 2021, sections III-B and V-B.

**4.4 Snapping.** Moving nearby nodes onto the tool surface gives a smooth cut with no new elements. It flattens elements: one study measured degenerate elements at about 5 percent of the cut faces (one test mesh) and a tenfold solver slow-down when they stay. A bounded rule exists: snap a node only if no tet becomes degenerate. Sources: Nienhuys and van der Stappen 2001, section 3.5; Steinemann, Harders, Gross, Szekely 2006, section 4.5. The task prompt's example, Steinemann, Otaduy, Gross 2006, is a meshless method.

**4.5 Subdivision.** Splitting each cut tet into smaller tets adds 4 to 17 elements per cut element, creates tiny elements near nodes, and changes the coloring graph. Sources: Mor and Kanade 2000, sections 3 and 4; Yu et al. 2025, section 4.3.

**4.6 Deletion.** Removing the elements that the tool hits is the simplest method. It loses volume and leaves a gap one element wide. Source: Wu et al. 2014, section 2.2.2. Inference: a gap of one element is the only start state in this list that is not at distance zero, but the two faces no longer match, so closing the wound with stitches leaves strain.

**4.7 Contact at the start.** Every volume-keeping cut creates its two faces at the same coordinates. The papers give four answers. Molino ignores interfering triangles and perturbs. Two papers take the penetration depth from a signed distance in the rest shape, with the side decided by material connectivity. One barrier-contact paper gives a closed interface a virtual start gap (0.376 of the barrier distance). One surgery system (cut, then suture) skips tissue-tissue collision. No source found uses a penalty contact with a finite contact distance on two coincident faces. Sources: Molino et al. 2004, section 6.2; Wu, Dick, Westermann 2013, section 6; Mitchell, Aanjaneya, Setaluri, Sifakis 2015, section 3; Zhao et al. 2022 (arXiv 2107.05814); Mitchell, Cutting, Sifakis 2015 (GRIDiron), section 4.2; `study/verify-literature.md`.

## 5. Lessons from the earlier cut notes

These notes describe the rejected work. They are used as lessons only.

1. The rebuild followed from unknowns and tets that grow at run time with no spare capacity. The notes call it a prototype cost and record no attempt to edit in place. `vbd-xfem-surgical-cut/design.md:13-15`, `vbd-xfem-surgical-cut/design.md:205-206`
2. The full cut never passed: the best run cut 148 of 150 elements. No recorded failure names the rebuild as its cause. `vbd-xfem-surgical-cut/design.md:3-5`
3. New faces were born at the same place. They crossed on the first closing move and stayed crossed. `vbd-xfem-surgical-cut/native-zero-gap-design.md:5-8`
4. That work forbade a birth offset, pair lists and a second force law, by its own policy. Its replacement ended with truncation off. The notes show no test of the simple fixes. `vbd-xfem-surgical-cut/native-zero-gap-assessment.md:55-56`

6. With an embedded collision surface, contact records no longer held particle ids. `vbd-xfem-surgical-cut/integration-notes.md:106-108`
7. The cut trigger tested whole tets against a world-fixed plane at sampled times, with no history of material motion. Tissue that moved between two calls left uncut bridges. ## 6. What follows for the design

Each line is an inference from the sections named in brackets.

1. All capacity exists before capture: spare particles, spare triangle rows, spare edge rows, contact buffer rows. No array is replaced or resized. [2.5, 2.9, 2.10]
2. A cut edits contents only: tet corners, adjacency records, masses, flags, triangle and edge rows. [2.1, 2.2, 2.5]
3. A new vertex needs a slot in a color group that exists before capture, and both color arrays must agree. [2.3]
4. Facing vertices of the two cut faces should not share a color. [1.8]
5. New faces need a positive start gap with every vertex on its correct side, or a new rule for pairs at distance zero. [2.7, 5.3]
6. The n-ring filter lists and the rest-shape exclusion cannot be used as they are on a cut body. [2.6]
7. Unused spares must be parked away from live geometry and from every tool, or the detection kernels need a skip rule. [2.4]
8. Cut faces must be triangles of simulation particles, so that contact records, the suture gate and the puncture keep one index space. [3.5, 5.6]
9. For the stitch, each cut face must become a boundary in the suture's tet-neighbor table, and the vertex-to-tet table must be right for new vertices. [1.5]
10. For the stitch on a closed cut, the wound must be open by more than the gate radius where the needle crosses, or the gate needs a side test. [1.6]
11. A blade that enters the tissue needs a contact switch of its own; the suture gate is not general. [1.6, 2.11]
12. The cut front moves by whole elements. [3.3, 5.10]
13. The cut trigger must follow the blade relative to the material and keep its marks. [5.7]
14. Existing particle and tet indices never change. [1.5, 5.6]
